# Scientific design

## Exact soft targets

Let critic index `c=0` denote objective reward, `c=1` speed cost, and `c=2`
deadline cost. Let `m_c(s,a,s')` be the corresponding transition metric, `gamma=.99`,
and

```text
Vbar_c(s; epsilon) = min_i Qbar_c,i(s, tanh(mu(s)+sigma(s)*epsilon))
                     - alpha log pi(a|s).
```

The same sampled squashed-Gaussian actor and the same entropy subtraction are used for
all three critics because that is the pinned FSRL behavior. Model-rollout Gaussian
noise comes only from a dedicated NumPy generator whose full state is checkpointed.

For a nonterminal replay prefix, the two real metrics are `m^R_t,c` and
`m^R_t+1,c`. Starting at the real successor state `s_t+2`, sample

```text
a_t+2+j ~ pi(. | imagined_observation(s_t+2+j)),  j = 0,...,M-1,
```

and advance exact physics to obtain `m^P_j,c`. The target is

```text
y_t,c = m^R_t,c + gamma m^R_t+1,c
        + sum[j=0..M-1] gamma^(2+j)
            (m^P_j,c - alpha log pi(a_t+2+j | s_t+2+j))
        + gamma^(2+M)
            (min_i Qbar_c,i(s_t+2+M,a*) - alpha log pi(a*|s_t+2+M)),

a* ~ pi(. | imagined_observation(s_t+2+M)).
```

Thus the first 0.2 seconds always come from real replay; they are never regenerated by
physics. The entropy term begins at the policy value of `s_t+2`, exactly where the
pinned two-step target would otherwise bootstrap. This expansion applies identically
to objective, speed-cost and deadline-cost critics, preserving critic order.

If a real or imagined transition truly terminates the route, its transition metric is
included and every later term is zero. A finite-horizon truncation includes its metric,
stops expansion, and bootstraps the target critic from its next observation, matching
the pinned FSRL `value_mask = not terminated` behavior. An unfinished replay-buffer
edge is treated like the pinned two-step calculation: reward traversal stops and a
target value is retained. No simulation crosses either boundary.

At `M=0`, the adapter does not reconstruct this expression. It invokes untouched
pinned `policy.update`, making the parity condition exact by construction and testable
against the frozen learner.

## Frozen task signals

The task remains Constrained RL V2:

```text
objective_t = -signed_step_energy_kwh / 0.25
speed_cost_t = 0.1 * max(0, velocity_t - speed_limit_t)
T_min(x) = optimistic remaining speed-limit-respecting travel time
slack = 140 - elapsed_time - T_min(position)
deficit = max(0, -slack)
deadline_cost_t = 0.1 * deficit / 140
```

Both cost limits are zero. There is no reward shaping, speed margin, braking penalty,
progress reward, terminal bonus, or PID change. External `EpisodeMetrics` and
`is_feasible` remain evaluation authority.

## Physical horizon derivation

The generator permits limits from 20 to 100 km/h and constrains an adjacent change to
at most 40 km/h. Native `dt` is 0.1 s, the public preview is 150 m, and legal action
`-1` requests maximum braking. Repeated calls to the exact
`domain.dynamics.advance` from 100 km/h show:

```text
largest legal single drop: 100 -> 60 km/h
first state at or below target: step 60
T_brake_max: 6.0 s
D_brake_max: 135.0764556315701 m
final sampled speed: 16.603915137238552 m/s
```

Therefore the smallest native horizon covering the relevant worst-case single legal
transition is preregistered as `M=ceil(6.0/0.1)=60`. It is not a performance-selected
or conventionally rounded deep-RL horizon. The model portion is 6.0 s; with the frozen
real prefix the nominal critic target spans 62 steps or 6.2 s. The 135.08 m braking
distance fits within the 150 m public preview.

For context only, two consecutive maximum legal drops can produce 100 to 20 km/h.
Exact maximum braking takes 100 steps, 10.0 s and 179.8249471715907 m. That is not one
legal adjacent generator transition and exceeds public preview, so it is not used to
inflate M.

## Physics and observability

The batched path implements the same equations as `domain.dynamics.advance` and reuses
the validated scalar `TrackContext` projection for observation, objective, costs,
metrics, completion and time limit. It is parity-tested at batch sizes 1, 7, and 32.

The actor always receives only the ordinary eight-dimensional normalized observation:
current kinematics, current/visible future limits and distances, and energy factor. It
never receives route position, clock, the full track map, or model state.

Target construction is asymmetric and privileged. At an imagined later state, limit
features entering that state's ordinary observation are information the deployed actor
would legitimately observe after physically advancing. However, constructing that
state and querying track segments beyond the source state's 150 m view requires the
benchmark-internal `ModelState` and full `TrackContext`. Consequently this is
privileged training information in a partially observable actor setting, not a pure
observation-only world model. It may affect learned critics and thereby the actor, but
the actor input and actor-only evaluation remain unchanged.

## Method boundary

The current primary method is multi-step exact-physics value expansion during critic
training. The completed H=1 method was Dyna-style synthetic replay augmentation.
Online MPC/CEM/TD-MPC2 would optimize imagined action sequences for control; none is
implemented or called here.
